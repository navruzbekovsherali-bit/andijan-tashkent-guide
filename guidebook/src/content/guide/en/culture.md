Uzbekistan is a country where relationships between people often matter more than schedules. These few rules will help you ease into local life.

## Hospitality

A guest here is a blessing. If you are invited for tea, do not rush to say no.

- **Tea.** The bowl (piala) is not filled to the brim: this way the host can top up your tea more often and show care. A full bowl hints that it is time for the guest to leave.
- **A small gift.** Do not come to someone's home empty-handed. Fruit, sweets or flatbread are the best way to show respect.

## Mahalla

A mahalla is not just a neighbourhood but a community where everyone knows each other.

- **Say hello first.** Even with a neighbour you do not know, a simple "Assalomu alaykum" immediately wins people over.
- **Mutual help.** Neighbours share food and help each other in hard times.

## Bread (non)

Bread is sacred here — a symbol of prosperity and well-being.

- Flatbread is not cut with a knife but broken by hand, and it is not placed face down.
- Bread is never thrown away. If you see a piece on the ground, pick it up and put it somewhere clean, for example on a ledge of a wall.

## Respect for elders

This is the foundation of society: age here means wisdom.

- Elders are given the best seat at the table and a seat on public transport.
- Do not interrupt elders or argue with them in public.

## The pace of life

Important matters here are settled in an unhurried conversation.

- **People first, then business.** Before a business conversation it is customary to ask about the other person's health, family and how things are going.
- **Patience.** Deadlines can sometimes be flexible, but relationships always come first.

> **Tip.** A sincere smile and a couple of words in Uzbek will help you settle in faster than any history book.
