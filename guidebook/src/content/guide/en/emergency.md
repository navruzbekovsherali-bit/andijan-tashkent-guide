Bookmark this page or add the numbers to your phone right now. Keep them close at hand, even if you never need them.

## Emergency services

All services work 24 hours a day. If something goes wrong, call without hesitating.

| Number | Service | When to call |
|---|---|---|
| **112** | Single emergency service | Any emergency |
| **101** | Fire service | Fire |
| **102** | Police (militsiya) | Threat to safety, an offence |
| **103** | Ambulance | Threat to life or health |
| **104** | Gas emergency service | Smell of gas, an accident |
| **1050** | Ministry of Emergency Situations (МЧС) | Man-made and natural disasters |

> **Tip.** In Tashkent and other large cities, 112 operators often understand Russian and English. Be ready to explain briefly what has happened and where you are, or ask people nearby to help.

## Bank contacts

Ask your onboarding specialist for the contacts of your HR Business Partner, the bank's emergency line, the office address and working hours. Save them in your phone on your very first day.

## Your support

There are always people nearby who are ready to help.

- **Onboarding specialist:** helps you settle in and answers any questions in your first months.
- **Buddy (mentor):** your guide to the company culture. Ask your onboarding specialist how a Buddy is assigned and how to reach them.
- **Your manager:** for all work questions.

## Good to know

1. **Registration.** Foreign citizens must be registered at their place of stay. Make sure yours is done on time. There is more on the "Your first 72 hours after arrival" page.
2. **Health insurance.** Emergency ambulance care is free, but for treatment in private clinics it is better to have voluntary health insurance (ДМС). Check your benefits package at the bank.
3. **Government services.** Install the MyGov app ([my.gov.uz](https://my.gov.uz)). There you can check the status of your documents and get many certificates online.
4. **Safety.** Uzbekistan is one of the safest countries, but the basic rules still apply: do not leave your belongings unattended, and respect local traditions.
