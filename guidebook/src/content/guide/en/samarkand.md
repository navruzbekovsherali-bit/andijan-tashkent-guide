Samarkand is one of the oldest cities in the world, as old as Rome. Here madrasahs with turquoise domes stand next to busy bazaars that smell of flatbread and spices.

## How to get there

1. **The Afrosiyob train** — the most convenient way from Tashkent. The journey takes about **2 h 10 min – 2 h 30 min**.
2. **By plane** — Samarkand has a modern airport with flights from Tashkent and abroad.
3. **By car or taxi** — about 4 hours. On the way you can stop in Jizzakh and try the famous Jizzakh samsa.

> **Tip.** Buy train tickets in advance, ideally 4–6 weeks ahead, on the official website [eticket.railway.uz](https://eticket.railway.uz). They sell out fast.

## What to see

### Registan Square

The heart of the city: an ensemble of three madrasahs — Ulugbek, Sher-Dor and Tilya-Kori. Go inside Tilya-Kori to see the golden dome. In the evening there is often a light show here.

### Shah-i-Zinda

An ancient necropolis that looks like an endless corridor of blue and turquoise buildings. No two tile patterns here are the same.

### Gur-e-Amir

The mausoleum of Amir Temur (Tamerlane) with a huge ribbed blue dome.

### Bibi-Khanym Mosque

Once one of the largest mosques in the Islamic world. It amazes with its size and with the legend of the ruler's love for his wife.

## Food and walks

- **Plov Centre (Panjob).** Come by 12:00. Samarkand plov is not stirred while cooking, so it is light and juicy.
- **Siab Bazaar** next to Bibi-Khanym. People buy Samarkand flatbread here (it stays fresh for a long time), halva and dried apricots.
- **Konigil and the "Eternal City" tourist complex.** Here you can see paper being made from mulberry bark using the old technique.

Getting around the city is easy by taxi with Yandex Go. It is best to look for a hotel in the old town, near the Registan.

## A plan for one day

1. In the morning — the **Registan**, before the crowds arrive.
2. For lunch — **Samarkand plov**.
3. In the evening — a walk around **Shah-i-Zinda** at sunset.
